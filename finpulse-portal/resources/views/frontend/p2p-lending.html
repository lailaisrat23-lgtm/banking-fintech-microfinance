Marketplace overview

What a marketplace would do — and what it would not

A marketplace matches people willing to lend money with small businesses seeking funding. The platform would typically handle listing, disclosure, payment administration and record-keeping.

It would not guarantee repayment, insure the lender against default or turn a lending arrangement into a deposit product. Those distinctions are the whole point of this page.

Borrower journey (concept)

  1. Describe the need. Purpose, amount and expected income that would support repayment.
  2. Publish a summary. A structured profile would be shown to prospective lenders.
  3. Review the terms. Rate, term, frequency and total cost stated in writing.
  4. Repay on schedule. Payments tracked and visible to both sides.

No borrower profile on this demonstration is real, and no application can be submitted.

Lender journey (concept)

  1. Understand the risk. Review default risk, concentration risk and liquidity limits.
  2. Set a limit. Decide the maximum exposure that could be lost entirely.
  3. Diversify. Spread contributions across many small amounts rather than one large one.
  4. Track and reassess. Monitor repayment records and adjust expectations.

No investment product is offered and no funds can be transferred.

Risk disclosure

Where the money can be lost

A lending marketplace is not a savings account. These are the risks any honest disclosure would have to state.

Default risk

A borrower may fail to repay some or all of the amount. Lenders can lose their entire contribution.

Concentration risk

Putting a large share into one opportunity makes a single default far more damaging.

Liquidity risk

Money lent is usually committed for the term. There may be no way to exit early.

Information risk

Listed information may be incomplete or inaccurate, and past repayment behaviour does not predict the future.

No protection scheme

Marketplace lending is generally not covered by deposit insurance or compensation arrangements.

Conduct and platform risk

A platform could fail, change its terms or mishandle records. Governance and legal status matter.

Diversification

Why spreading exposure matters

Diversification reduces the impact of any single default. It does not remove the risk of loss.

Consider two fictional lenders each contributing BDT 60,000. The first puts everything into one opportunity; the second splits it across twelve. If one borrower defaults, the first loses everything, while the second loses roughly one twelfth — before considering recovery.

Illustrative concentration example (fictional amounts)
Approach Contributions Amount per opportunity Exposure to one default
Concentrated 1 BDT 60,000 100% of capital
Moderately spread 6 BDT 10,000 ≈ 17% of capital
Broadly spread 12 BDT 5,000 ≈ 8% of capital

Loss is always possible

Even a broadly spread portfolio can lose money if many borrowers default at once, for example during an economic shock affecting a whole sector or region.

Never lend money you need

Do not commit funds needed for rent, school fees, medical costs or emergencies. Lending capital should be money you could afford to lose entirely.

Ask about governance

Who holds the money, how are records kept, what happens if the platform stops operating, and which regulator, if any, oversees it?
