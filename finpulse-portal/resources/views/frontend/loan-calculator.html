How the estimate works

Assumptions behind the calculation

  • The annual interest rate is converted to a periodic rate by dividing it by the number of instalments per year: 12 for monthly, 26 for fortnightly and 52 for weekly.
  • The term is entered in months and converted to a number of instalments using the same frequency. A 12-month weekly loan therefore has 52 instalments.
  • The instalment is calculated with the standard amortisation formula: P Ã— i Ã· (1 âˆ’ (1 + i)âˆ’n), where P is the amount, i the periodic rate and n the number of instalments.
  • When the annual rate is zero, the amount is divided by the number of instalments and interest is reported as BDT 0.
  • Results are rounded to whole Taka for display, so the sum of the displayed instalments may differ from the displayed total by a few Taka.
  • Processing fees, taxes, insurance, late-payment charges and early-settlement adjustments are all excluded.
  • No compounding convention beyond per-period interest on the remaining balance is modelled, and no grace period is applied.

This is not an offer

The calculator cannot approve anything and does not know your income, expenses or credit history. A real provider would assess affordability before offering terms.

Interpreting the numbers

A larger instalment usually means a shorter, cheaper loan. A smaller instalment usually means a longer, more expensive one. Compare the total estimated repayment column before choosing.

Zero-interest example

Enter BDT 12,000 at 0% over 12 months monthly. The result is twelve instalments of BDT 1,000 with no interest, and the total equals the amount borrowed.

Questions

Calculator questions

Real agreements often add processing fees, insurance or taxes to the financed amount, use a different day-count convention, or apply a fixed-rate rather than reducing-balance method. Those differences change the instalment.

With more frequent payments the balance falls sooner, so less interest accrues over the same term. The difference is usually small compared with fees, which are excluded here.
